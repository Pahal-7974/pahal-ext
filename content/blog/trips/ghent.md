---
title: A day at the Gentse Feesten (Ghent, BE)
---
Some day trips need a plan. This one just needed a full tank of gas and an appetite for wandering.

We set off in the morning for the drive to Ghent, rolling into the city right around noon. First lesson of the day: the free P&R car parks fill up fast. We ended up parking at Ledeburg instead and hopping on the tram into the centrum, arriving right in time for the Gentse Feesten.

## Into the Festival

The plan was to start at the Belfry, but a small mishap there (no clue what this was btw, did someone fall??? we didn't see anyone, so hopefully not) meant we gave it a miss and let the day pull us elsewhere instead. We swung by the Mardi Gras concert for a bit, grabbed a mirror selfie at a tiny photo booth tucked into the festival crowd, and started making our way toward the castle.

Ghent has a way of throwing little surprises at you mid-walk. A shop selling Indian-inspired trinkets caught our eye. So did a Neuhaus and a Fred Au Merveilleux, both filed away under "we'll stop here on the way back." There was also, memorably, a giant inflated duck bobbing somewhere in the festival chaos, and a row of Delirium beer elephants floating above a hotel tower like they owned the skyline.

## The Castle Ticket Shuffle

The bridge over the river on the way to the castle was genuinely one of the prettiest moments of the day, the kind of view that makes you stop mid-conversation just to look.

The castle itself, though, was fully booked for the next slot, so we grabbed tickets for later instead. Small hiccup: the online ticket portal wouldn't accept Visa, so despite booking "online," we still ended up queuing at the desk to sort it out in person. Worth remembering for next time.

## Lunch at Barouche

With time to kill before our castle slot, we headed to Barouche for some Mediterranean fast food. We signed up for their membership on the spot and got free drinks out of it, then ordered the Berloumi bowl.

Verdict: completely worth it. It was so filling that we shared just the one bowl between us (and free drinks with the membership) and called it lunch, no regrets..

## Inside the Castle

Then, the castle. The audio guide turned out to be the highlight, narrated with a genuinely captivating story, if a little long-winded in places. The castle itself is all stairs, up and down and up again, and between that and the extra-long audio notes we took our time.

The staff mentioned the average visit runs 30 to 60 minutes. We'd assumed we'd land closer to the 30-minute end. We clocked in at an hour and a half. In our defense, we were stopping at every vantage point for photos and sitting down after every little burst of stairs.

## Back to the Sweets

Walking back into the city, we stopped for a souvenir before retracing our steps to Fred Au Merveilleux, the place we'd marked out hours earlier.

We tried their signature cocktail, expecting it to be boozy since one of us was driving, so we only ordered one to share. Turns out the name was misleading. No alcohol at all. We could have easily ordered two. Alongside it came mini merveilleux, which were fantastic, and we picked up a few meringue cookies from the café as a final edible souvenir. Also fantastic.

## Heading Home

And that was Ghent: one drive there, one drive back, close enough to make a full day of it without it feeling rushed. Festival chaos, a slightly-too-thorough castle visit, one incredible bowl of food, and a cocktail with a false reputation. Would do it all again, minus the Visa card drama at the castle desk.
